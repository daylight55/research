## Evidence notes

- Israel uses a parliamentary system with proportional representation, so coalition bargaining matters more than a simple left-right seat count.
- Judicial reform is not a side issue. It changes appointments, review power, and the distribution of authority inside the state.
- Gaza and the West Bank need separate treatment. They share a political frame, but their administrative and humanitarian realities differ.
- Iran belongs in the report as a military, nuclear, and proxy threat together, not as a single-country rivalry.
- The U.S. relationship is the base layer of Israel's external strategy because it ties together aid, cover, and technical cooperation.
- Gulf normalization broadens economics and diplomacy, but it does not erase the Palestinian issue.
- High tech remains a strength, but wartime mobilization and political uncertainty still feed back into hiring and investment.

## Downgraded sources

- Opinion columns and general explainers were useful for tone, but they were too loose for institutional claims.
- Wikipedia was left out of the evidence base.
- Fast-moving battlefield reports were kept out of the center of the article because they age too quickly.

## Open questions

- How stable the coalition will be after the next judicial or security decision.
- Whether Gaza and West Bank conditions will shift U.S. and Gulf policy.
- How much defense spending and mobilization can continue before they pinch growth and labor supply.
