## Starting points and selection

Examples were selected where the nipple connects to material, light, natural bodily appearance, education, desire, touch, publication rules, or usefulness. Museum and heritage records support object information; scholarly and curatorial accounts support interpretations; artist statements support project histories. The result is not an exhaustive inventory of visible nipples.

## Why these examples were included

- **Different materials.** The Nakht, Boxer, and Pompeian ephebe records explicitly describe nipple inlays. Bronze and copper work is distinguished from inlay in a wooden figure.
- **Light and contour.** Page 27 of the Adam paper supports the discussion of asymmetry. The account is scholarly interpretation, not the sculptor's statement.
- **Faith and bodies.** Saint Sebastian concerns sensuality in the entire nude. Kannon supplies natural bodily expression and Fudō Myōō the possibility of clothing; bodhisattvas are not fixed within modern male categories.
- **Learning and looking.** Gillot and Kuroda sources establish educational contexts. Mapplethorpe object identification is separated from interpretation of his broader photographic work.
- **Touch and rules.** Cold Breath uses an exhibition record and Hebron an artist interview. Readings of viewer sensation are not presented as experimental results.
- **Use and value.** Takasaki's qualification and the gallery statement are both retained, avoiding a biological assertion that male nipples lack function.

## Exclusions and weaknesses

The presence of a depicted nipple alone cannot establish an artist's sexual intent or a specific symbol. This selection centers on Western art with ancient Egyptian and Japanese examples; it is not a global or regional census. In the earlier investigation, the Boxer account, Adam paper, and Saint Sebastian record were read on the Web, but direct file archiving failed. This public note retains their links and verification scope. Hebron's project and statements concern 2014–15, rather than current platform policy or a moderation workaround.

## Evidence inventory

### MIHO MUSEUM

[Statue of Nakht](https://www.miho.jp/booth/html/artcon/00000933.htm)

Museum object account, around 1960–1916 BCE. Inlaid eyes and nipples and the interpretation of the ka statue.

### The Metropolitan Museum of Art 2013

[The Boxer: An Ancient Masterpiece](https://www.metmuseum.org/exhibitions/listings/2013/the-boxer)

Museum exhibition account of the Boxer and copper inlays. Web text examined in the earlier investigation; direct archival download failed. The dating remains a range.

### J. Paul Getty Museum 2009–2013

[Roman Ephebe from Naples](https://www.getty.edu/art/exhibitions/naples_ephebe/)

Museum conservation account of the Roman ephebe, around 20–10 BCE. Copper lips and nipples identified through conservation.

### Luke Syson & Valeria Cafà 2014

[Adam by Tullio Lombardo](https://resources.metmuseum.org/resources/metpublications/pdf/Adam_by_Tullio_Lombardo_Metropolitan_Museum_Journal_v_49_2014.pdf)

Metropolitan Museum Journal 49, pages 9–31, especially page 27. Scholarly interpretation of nipple asymmetry, contour, and light. Web text examined; direct PDF archiving failed. Not an artist statement.

### The Metropolitan Museum of Art

[Saint Sebastian, Circle of Master of the Furies](https://www.metmuseum.org/art/collection/search/239276)

Museum interpretation of a seventeenth-century ivory Saint Sebastian. Web text examined; direct archiving failed. Discusses sympathy and sensuality of the whole nude, not a nipple-specific symbol.

### National Institutes for Cultural Heritage, e-Museum

[Standing Kannon Bosatsu, Hōryūji donation](https://emuseum.nich.go.jp/detail?content_base_id=100758&content_part_id=000&content_pict_id=0&langId=ja&webView=)

Official object account, seventh century, Tokyo National Museum N-184. Nipples described as part of natural bodily expression.

### Agency for Cultural Affairs

[Wooden Standing Fudō Myōō, formerly Saionji goma hall principal image](https://online.bunka.go.jp/db/heritages/detail/207520)

Official cultural heritage account of the 1225 Fudō Myōō. Describes nipples and the possibility of actual clothing in a context of living-presence devotion.

### Cleveland Museum of Art

[Claude Gillot, Seated Male Nude](https://www.clevelandart.org/art/2008.353)

Museum explanation of Gillot's Seated Male Nude, 1693–95, and academic life drawing. Supports the training context, not a universal rule for nipple representation.

### Tokyo National Museum 2012

[Artistic Anatomy: Learning the Human Form](https://www.tnm.jp/modules/r_exhibition/index.php?controller=item&id=3335&lang=ja)

Museum exhibition account of artistic anatomy, including Kuroda's 1889 half-length male nude and anatomical studies made in France.

### J. Paul Getty Museum 2012–2013

[In Focus: Robert Mapplethorpe](https://www.getty.edu/art/exhibitions/focus_mapplethorpe/)

Museum interpretation of Mapplethorpe's classical formal interests and homoerotic content. Concerns his male nudes as a group.

### Robert Mapplethorpe Foundation 2017

[Xavier Hufkens: Robert Mapplethorpe](https://www.mapplethorpe.org/exhibitions/xavier-hufkens)

Foundation exhibition record identifying Nipple, 1988. Object identification, not a definitive artist explanation of its meaning.

### Tobias Ostrander / Robert Mapplethorpe Foundation 2021

[Mapplethorpe: Escultura](https://www.mapplethorpe.org/exhibitions/moran-moran/about)

Foundation exhibition text connecting bodily lighting, cropped compositions, and sculpture. Used for broader context, not as a statement exclusively about Nipple.

### ART iT 2016

[Steve McQueen @ RAT HOLE GALLERY](https://www.art-it.asia/u/admin_ed_pics/hnrp9u0mpnsxzdai8kw7/)

Record of McQueen's Cold Breath, 1999, exhibited in Japan in 2016. Describes contact, duration, projection, and viewer embodiment.

### Chapman University 2015

[Male Nipple Template, #FreetheNipple and Micol Hebron Go Viral](https://news.chapman.edu/2015/07/22/male-nipple-template-to-freethenipple-and-micol-hebron-go-viral/)

Artist interview published by Chapman University in 2015 about a project begun in 2014. Supports the artist's account of removal and the template; no present-day posting guarantee.

### Teiya Iwabuchi / Bijutsu Techo 2020

[Is functionality value? Shohei Takasaki on male nipples](https://bijutsutecho.com/magazine/interview/22519)

2020 interview with Takasaki and Eri Takane. Describes five proposed uses and the artist's qualification that he did not define male nipples as functionless.

### OIL by Bijutsu Techo 2020

[Shohei Takasaki: sun, snake, nipples, curated by Eri Takane](https://oil-gallery.bijutsutecho.com/exhibition/shohei-takasaki%E3%80%8Csun-snake-nipples%E3%80%8Dcurated-by-eri-takane/)

Hosting gallery's exhibition record and curatorial statement. Supports the question of usefulness and value, rather than an anatomical claim.

## Image rights and role

The hero is an archival image whose CC0 status was checked against the [Cleveland Museum of Art record](https://www.clevelandart.org/art/2008.353) and official collection API. It illustrates historical anatomy or artistic education, rather than supplying experimental evidence for biological claims. The binding plot in the companion science article is calculated from a general equation, not measured animal or human data.
