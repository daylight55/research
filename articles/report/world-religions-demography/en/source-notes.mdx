# Source Notes: World Religions by Population

## Scope

- The report covers global religious population distribution, regional concentration, diversity, and change from 2010 to 2020.
- The baseline is Pew Research Center's 2025 estimates for 2010 and 2020, plus Pew's 2026 religious diversity analysis.
- World population totals follow the UN World Population Prospects 2024 totals used by Pew.
- The 2050 discussion uses Pew's 2015 projection only as an older scenario, because Pew revised its 2010 baseline in the 2025 release.

## Inclusion Decisions

- The report treats religious population as affiliation, not as regular practice or doctrinal belief.
- The "other religions" category is treated as an umbrella category that includes Baha'i, Daoist, Jain, Shinto, Sikh, Wiccan, Zoroastrian, folk, and traditional religious identities.
- The article uses "religiously unaffiliated" rather than "atheist" or "nonreligious" because some unaffiliated people retain religious or spiritual practices.
- Denominational differences, doctrinal disputes, and church-state arrangements remain outside this report's scope.
- The report does not recalculate 2020 affiliation shares using the 2026 global population.

## Weak Points

- Pew's downloadable dataset required an account login, so the report relies on publicly visible report pages, methodology summaries, the interactive table page, and Our World in Data.
- In China, Japan, South Korea, and other societies, institutional religious affiliation does not capture every ritual or cultural practice.
- Census wording, response options, and social pressure differ by country.
- Pew's 2015 projection predates the 2025 revisions, so the report avoids presenting its 2050 figures as updated estimates.
