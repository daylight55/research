## Evidence that shaped the article

- The monarchy and consultative structure were grounded in the official government description and the appointed Shura Council.
- Vision 2030 was treated as an official roadmap, with the 2024 annual report used for non-oil growth, tourism, women’s employment, and export progress.
- The 2023 Saudi-Iran joint statement anchored the détente story, while the article preserved the fact that rivalry has not disappeared.
- The U.S. relationship was written as a long security partnership that is now more conditional around oil policy, Gaza, and human rights.
- Post-Gaza normalization with Israel was kept conditional on Palestinian state recognition.
- Population and labor questions were combined from GASTAT population estimates and rights reports on labor and speech restrictions.

## Downgraded sources

- Wikipedia was used only for quick orientation, not for institutional claims or current relations.
- Opinion columns and fast-moving news summaries were not used as the main basis for the system explanation.
- Secondary articles with stale data were excluded when the underlying official source was available.

## Remaining questions

- Vision 2030 outcomes still need to be checked against independent assessments, not only official progress reporting.
- The Iran thaw may remain stable, but proxy risks and sea-lane risks can still reappear quickly.
- Labor reform needs field verification, not just legal text.
- Public information is not enough to settle the long-term balance of power in succession politics.
