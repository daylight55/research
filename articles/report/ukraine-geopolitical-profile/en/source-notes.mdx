## Evidence Notes

- After the full-scale invasion, Ukraine is best read as a wartime state, not just as a state at war, because national capacity has been redirected toward defense.
- Election delay under martial law is better described as wartime governance than as a simple institutional pause, because fighting, displacement, occupied territory, and election logistics all weaken normal voting conditions.
- EU accession talks, NATO support, reconstruction funding, and anti-corruption reform belong to one conditionality bundle rather than separate policy tracks.
- Civilian life includes outages, displacement, job insecurity, and housing pressure, so the war cannot be read only through battlefield headlines.
- Language and identity are both a fault line and a cohesion indicator, especially because support for independence and a European future remain strong.
- For Japan, the key issues are sanctions compliance, reconstruction participation, shipping and insurance risk, cyber defense, and deterrence toward China.

## Inclusion / Exclusion Decisions

- Front-line micro-tactics were left out because they belong closer to a war-status report than to a country profile.
- The details of negotiations with Russia were reduced to the overlap between territory and sovereignty because the diplomatic record changes too quickly.
- Public-opinion polling was used as a supporting line, not as a basis for sweeping claims about the entire population.
- Anti-corruption was framed as an enabling condition for EU accession and financing, not as a list of scandals.

## Open Questions

- How long will wartime law continue, and in what order will election conditions reappear?
- How fast will EU accession clusters move?
- How much will return migration and outward flight reshape the labor market?
- Will European orientation and language practice stay stable under long-war fatigue?
