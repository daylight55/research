### Authoritative data and supporting context

- Statistics Canada is the main source for population and migration pressure.
- CMHC is the main source for housing supply and affordability pressure.
- Natural Resources Canada is the main source for forests and critical-minerals data.

### Secondary context

- CSIS public reporting was used for interference risk and community impact.
- Global Affairs Canada and Finance Canada briefing material were used for current U.S. and Arctic framing.

## Evidence Notes

- Federalism and minority government together explain why domestic bargaining matters for foreign policy.
- The 2025 election result is the key data point for current power distribution.
- Population estimates and the IRCC levels plan together show a managed migration phase rather than open-ended expansion.
- CMHC’s supply-gap estimates show that housing is a supply-capacity issue as much as a price issue.
- The Arctic foreign-policy paper, NATO 2% achievement, and the 2026 Arctic Allies statement show that the Arctic has become a security file.
- CSIS’s 2025 report and the renewed Canada-India channel show that China and India are security and domestic-trust files, not only trade files.
- Critical minerals and forestry need to be read as one bundle of resources, climate, trade, and security.

## Downgraded Material

- Daily polling and speculative cabinet rumors were excluded because they do not support a durable country profile.
- City-by-city crime data and local housing-price rankings were excluded because they exceed the scope of the piece.
- Secondary reporting on China and India was not used where primary government or CSIS sources were available.
