# Global Democratization: Country Records and Evidence Notes

## Scope, selection, and limitations

The country scope comprises 195 entries: the 193 UN member states plus the Holy See and Palestine. Taiwan and Kosovo are included separately for comparison. UN status is distinguished from governments' positions on state recognition. Overseas territories, autonomous territories, and historical states are discussed within relevant country records rather than counted again as present-day independent countries.<SourceNote>[UN Library: Current members](https://research.un.org/en/unmembers/currentmembers), [Non-member observers](https://research.un.org/en/unmembers/observers)</SourceNote>

The country records distinguish historical turning points, causes and participants, and outcomes and limits. The central comparative cases also examine preceding developments, organization, state responses, immediate political changes, and longer-term institutional changes. The selection explains the establishment, extension, defense, and reversal of democracy; it is not an event database of every demonstration worldwide.

Short country entries and detailed case studies do not have equal depth. Annual country reports help establish recent institutional conditions, while historical detail draws on archives, legislatures and courts, museums, academic research, and UN human-rights evidence. Government documents establish laws and decisions but do not necessarily provide neutral assessments of that government's conduct. Movement organizations' statements are also treated as claims by participants.

The cutoff date is October 21, 2026, Japan time. Publication years are distinguished from assessment periods, and French mobilization in October 2026 was checked against dated sources. The extent of current-event verification differs by country; the absence of a supplementary update does not establish that nothing changed.

## Democracy cannot be reduced to one score

International IDEA measures multiple dimensions: Representation, Rights, Rule of Law, and Participation. V-Dem's 2026 report identifies 87 democracies and 92 autocracies through its Regimes of the World classification for the end of 2025, but its 179 classified units and the 195-country scope here have different denominators. Annual indices do not determine the success or failure of ongoing demonstrations.<SourceNote>[International IDEA: Methodology 2026](https://www.idea.int/publications/catalogue/html/global-state-democracy-indices-technical-procedures-guide-version-10), [V-Dem: Democracy Report 2026](https://www.v-dem.net/documents/75/V-Dem_Institute_Democracy_Report_2026_lowres.pdf)</SourceNote>

Comparative research on nonviolent resistance identifies the importance of participant diversity, prior organization, cooperation, and state responses. Success rates vary over time, so neither a particular population percentage nor a single day's crowd size is treated as a universal condition for success. The explanations here include historical interpretations assembled from multiple sources.<SourceNote>[Erica Chenoweth: The Future of Nonviolent Resistance](https://www.journalofdemocracy.org/articles/the-future-of-nonviolent-resistance-2/)</SourceNote>

## Reading the country index

The entries below select at least one turning point for each country and compress its causes and outcomes. The main article examines major events comparatively in greater depth. This is not an equally detailed history of every country, and short entries sometimes draw on the historical and institutional sections of annual reports. More detailed causal judgments require further comparison with national archives and historical scholarship.

Freedom House's 2026 edition primarily assesses 2025, while BTI 2026 generally covers February 2023 through January 2025. Neither is used indiscriminately as evidence of conditions in October 2026. Familiar country names are preferred; the Holy See and Vatican City are distinguished but covered in one entry. XK/XKX is a practical code for Kosovo, not an official ISO assignment.

Taiwan and Kosovo are supplementary entries, not additions to the 195-country total. The tables therefore contain 197 rows. The presence of records and country counts can be checked mechanically, but those checks do not establish that the sources prove every aspect of the history.
