# Global Democratization and France: Research Method and Verification

- model: `gpt-5.4-mini`

## Research Instruction

Compare the history, causes, participants, state responses, immediate outcomes, and longer-term institutional changes of democracy, democratization, and major protest movements worldwide. Check 195 countries through a country index and examine events important to world history and national histories in greater depth. Assess the phase of France's current movement while distinguishing it from earlier protests, including those over pensions.

## Scope and assessment criteria

- Cutoff date: October 21, 2026, Japan time.
- Country scope: 193 UN member states, the non-member observers Holy See and Palestine, and Taiwan and Kosovo separately.
- Categories: distinguish democratization, defense of existing democracy, extension of rights, authoritarianization, and policy protest.
- Event selection: prioritize events that changed domestic institutions, influenced other countries, or allow comparison of the causes of failure and reversal.
- Causation: distinguish long-term conditions from immediate triggers, and treat policy concessions, a leader's departure, a change of government, and democratic consolidation as different outcomes.

## Cross-checking

The 193 country headings in the UN Library's current membership list were extracted and mapped to ISO codes. After combining regional records, checks compare the resulting set with the declared scope, detect duplicates, examine source URLs, and verify Japanese–English coverage. Matching country counts does not establish the completeness of the historical accounts. [UN Library: Current member states](https://research.un.org/en/unmembers/currentmembers)

The assessment period of an annual report, the date of an event, the publication date, and the access date were distinguished. Current French developments were checked against government and parliamentary records, statements by parties to the dispute such as unions, and independent reporting. Planned mobilization was not treated as completed action. Differences between organizers' and police attendance estimates were retained rather than converted into one definitive total.

## Approaches rejected

- Classifying every large demonstration as a democratization movement.
- Equating a dictator's departure with democratic consolidation.
- Presenting a single democracy index as covering all 195 countries.
- Treating the French pension protests of 2023 as the same movement as that of October 2026.

## Issues for continued review

Political institutions, protest participation, legislation, court proceedings, and elections change. The assessment of France describes the situation at the cutoff date; it does not predict the end of the movement or the collapse of the government. Country summaries vary in depth, and event selection, causal interpretation, and translation require human historical and language review.
